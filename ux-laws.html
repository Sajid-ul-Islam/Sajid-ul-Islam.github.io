Law #19

Law of Closure

The eye completes shapes that are cut off. When two cards fit the screen exactly, they look like the whole set. Let 32% of the next card peek through, and the cut edge says "there's more."

Law #20

Figure and Ground

The eye splits every screen into front and back. With no backdrop, the page behind a dialogue keeps 17.4:1 contrast and fights for attention. Dim it to 2.5:1, and the dialogue stands alone.

Law #21

Banner Blindness

People learn to skip anything that looks like an ad. Style a warning as a bright banner and the eye jumps right past it. Put it in the feed, styled like content, and it's read twice. Don't make important things look like ads.

Law #22

Parkinson's Law

A task swells to fill the time it's given, so shrink the task. Typing a full address takes 30 keys. Type the postcode, tap your address, and it's seven. Fill in what you already know.

Law #23

Uniform Connectedness

Things joined by a line read as one group. Four loose circles read as four separate things. Join them with one line and they become a single path with a start, end, and clear next step. Connect the steps of a flow.

Law #24

Choice Overload

More options feel like freedom until it's time to choose. In the famous jam study, a table of 24 jams sold to 3% of shoppers who stopped. Six jams, 30%. Show fewer options up front.
